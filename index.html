Thoracic limb4 joints · 1 sheath · 2 bursae
1
ShoulderScapulohumeral

Palpate

Cranial + caudal prominences of the greater tubercle and the notch between them; then the infraspinatus tendon — every approach is measured off it.

Needle

Spinal 18–20 G, 7–10 cm. Caudolateral, entering just caudal to the infraspinatus tendon, aimed craniomedially into the caudal recess.

Then

Second cannula into the same caudal recess. Arthroscopic reference: 1 cm caudal to the tendon border, 1 cm proximal to the caudal tubercle.

Lavage

1–2 L · two cannulas 1.6–2.0 mm · epidural or GA, not standing

Compartments

Simple joint — one cavity, no compartments. The intertubercular bursa is separate from the capsule, as in the horse, so a distended bursa is not a distended joint.

Watch out

Second hardest joint after the hip. Effusion is not palpable — capsule sits under thick muscle. Suprascapular nerve close. Never tap without ultrasound.

tap
Nuss et al., Arthrocentesis in cattle Pt 1, 2002 · Nuss, SAT 2003 · BMC Vet Res 2020 (arthroscopic approaches) · Budras p. 13
2
ElbowCubital

Palpate

Lateral humeral epicondyle, olecranon tip, and the lateral collateral ligament between them.

Needle

18 G, 5–7 cm. Enter just caudal to the caudal border of the lateral collateral ligament, directed craniomedially into the radiohumeral space.

Then

The caudomedial pouch — in sepsis this is not optional.

Lavage

1–2 L 0.9% NaCl

Compartments

One composite cavity. Humeroulnar, humeroradial and proximal radioulnar all share it — tap one, you are in all three. The distal radioulnar joint is absent (synostosis).

Watch out

Lavage from lateral alone leaves fibrin in the caudomedial pouch, and the joint then “fails to respond”.

tap
Nuss et al., Pt 1, 2002 · Borges et al., BMTW 2015 · Budras Fig. 12.1, p. 126
3
CarpusAntebrachiocarpal · middle · CMC

Palpate

Flex to 90°: the space opens as a dorsal depression just distal to the radius.

Effusion shows as filling of the palmar recess proximal to the accessory carpal bone.

Needle

18–20 G. Dorsal, in flexion, either side of the extensor tendons. Tap each compartment you want to assess separately.

Then

The palmar pouches — large, and where fibrin and pus collect.

Lavage

1–3 L · two cannulas 1.6–2.0 mm · IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

Three cavities. Antebrachiocarpal rarely communicates with the midcarpal; midcarpal and carpometacarpal always do. So the ABC behaves as its own compartment, and the lower two as one. The ABC cavity also includes the accessory carpal bone.

Watch out

Three joints, not one. Antebrachiocarpal rarely talks to the midcarpal; midcarpal and CMC always do. Lavaging one does not lavage the others.

tap
Nuss et al., Pt 1, 2002 · Desrochers et al., AJVR 1997 · Kofler & Martinek 2004 · Budras Fig. 12.2 and p. 127
4
FetlockMetacarpo-/metatarsophalangeal

Palpate

The dorsoproximal pouch bulges with even slight effusion, abaxial to the common digital extensor.

Needle

19 G. Dorsal: find the angle between the dorsal margin of P1 and the rounded metacarpal joint surface; insert abaxially, aimed at the axial surface, under the extensor tendon.

Then

Plantar triangle: bounded by the caudal margin of the cannon bone, the proximal edge of the abaxial sesamoid and the abaxial contour of the interosseus. Flex the fetlock, insert ~2 cm into its centre, axiodistally.

Lavage

1–2.5 L ± povidone-iodine 0.1%

Compartments

Two joints, one slit. Digits III and IV each have their own recess, joined at the incisura intercapitalis by a slit — wide enough for infection, too narrow to flush through. Multiple palmar/plantar pouches within each.

Watch out

Digits III and IV communicate through a slit at the incisura intercapitalis — wide enough for infection to cross, too narrow to flush both from one side.

tap
Budras Fig. 12.3 and p. 127 · Nuss et al., Pt 1, 2002 · Kofler & Martinek, Vet J 2005 · Hund, Senn & Kofler, Animals 2020
5
Carpal tendon sheathsExtensor + flexor sheaths

Palpate

Swelling runs along a tendon, above and below the carpus — not the dorsal joint-centred bulge.

Have sheaths: ext. carpi obliquus, digital extensors, flexor carpi radialis. Do not: ext. carpi radialis, ulnaris lateralis.

Needle

18–20 G, into the sonographically identified lumen at maximum distension, parallel to the tendon fibres.

Then

Second portal at the far end of the distended lumen.

Lavage

Small — lumen size dictates it. Septic cases usually need open debridement and drains.

Compartments

Separate from the joints entirely. Sheath lumina and joint pouches are distinct on ultrasound. Have sheaths: ext. carpi obliquus, the digital extensors, flexor carpi radialis. Do not: ext. carpi radialis, ulnaris lateralis.

Watch out

A swollen carpus has four causes needing four treatments: arthritis, tenosynovitis, precarpal hygroma, periarticular abscess. Scan each pouch and tendon separately.

tap
Kofler & Martinek, Tierärztl Prax 2004 (42 cattle) · Kofler, Vet Clin Food Anim 2009 · Budras p. 12
6
Precarpal bursa / hygromaBursa subcutanea praecarpalis

Palpate

Soft, fluctuant, superficial swelling on the dorsal carpus, from midcarpal joint to just below the metacarpal tuberosity.

Starts as hairless hyperkeratotic skin where cubicles are too short. Can reach the size of a man's head.

Needle

16–18 G into the fluctuant centre, aseptically. Submit for cytology and culture if infection is suspected.

Then

Before draining, prove it is extra-articular — ultrasound or contrast. It usually does not communicate, but “usually” is not a plan.

Lavage

Lameness is mild unless infected, then moderate to severe.

Compartments

Extra-articular, and usually does not communicate with anything beneath — but invasion into a sheath or the carpal joint does happen, rarely. Prove it with ultrasound or contrast before you drain.

Watch out

A bursa may fill because the joint or sheath under it is effused — then the bursa is the symptom. Image before you needle. At surgery, do not breach the synovial structures.

tap
Budras pp. 12, 126–127 · Kofler & Martinek, Tierärztl Prax 2004 · Seyrek-Intas et al., Vet Radiol Ultrasound 2005
7
Olecranon & acquired bursaeCapped elbow · presternal

Palpate

Firm or fluctuant swelling over the point of the elbow or the sternum. Pressure lesions, same mechanism as the carpal hygroma.

Subcutaneous olecranon bursa lies superficial on the caudal olecranon; the bursa of triceps brachii sits deeper, under the terminal tendon.

Needle

16–18 G, central aspiration after ultrasound confirms fluid. Expect viscous straw-coloured fluid if aseptic.

Lavage

Excision works well for the chronic fibrous form.

Compartments

Acquired subcutaneous cavities, no normal communication. The bursa of triceps brachii lies deeper, under the terminal tendon — a different structure from the subcutaneous one over the olecranon.

Watch out

Repeated drainage without fixing the lying surface achieves nothing and risks seeding it. Treat the cubicle.

tap
Budras pp. 12, 135 · Seyrek-Intas et al. 2005 · Kofler & Martinek 2004

PIP — pasternProximal interphalangeal

Palpate

Localised swelling between fetlock and coronary band. Usually follows a penetrating interdigital wound.

Needle

18–20 G short. Dorsoabaxial, needle perpendicular to the long axis of the pastern.

Then

Opposing needle, abaxial or axial.

Lavage

0.5–1 L until the efflux runs clear · IVRA with tourniquet

Compartments

The two pastern joints do not communicate with each other. But their dorsal pouches reach the coffin joint pouches — so PIP and DIP sepsis travel together far more readily than PIP to PIP.

Watch out

The flexor sheath runs directly behind — easy to tap it instead. The two pastern joints do not communicate with each other, but their dorsal pouches reach the coffin joint's.

tap
Kofler, BMTW 1995 · Desrochers & Francoz 2014 · Burgstaller & Kofler 2016 · Budras p. 12
2
DIP — coffinDistal interphalangeal

Palpate

Painful swelling at the caudal third of the abaxial coronet.

A draining tract at the dorsal coronary band is near-diagnostic. Grade 4–5 lame.

Needle

14 G × 5 cm, dorsoproximal to the coronary band, axial or abaxial to the extensor tendon, at 60° to the coronary band.

Then

Palmar/plantar to the abaxial collateral ligament.

Lavage

~1 L until clear and fibrin-free · IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

Its dorsal pouch connects with the pastern joint's. The navicular bursa sits immediately palmar between the DDFT and the navicular bone — a separate cavity, but deep digital sepsis rarely respects the boundary.

Watch out

Lavage suits only acute closed arthritis. Fibrin, pus or a penetrating wound → resection or amputation. Navicular bursa and DDFT sit immediately palmar.

tap
Desrochers & St-Jean, Vet Clin Food Anim 2008 · Heppelmann et al. 2009 · Starke et al., Vet Surg 2007 · Budras p. 27
3
Digital flexor tendon sheathDFTS

Palpate

Elongated swelling along the palmar/plantar fetlock, running proximally — not the round joint bulge.

Spans distal third of the cannon to the coronary band of the heel bulbs.

Needle

18 G. Abaxial, into the concavity palpable between the tendons and the interosseus with the fetlock flexed.

Then

Two compartments. Outer and inner proximally, joined only in the common distal compartment — so closed irrigation is difficult to impossible. Plan open debridement and drainage.

Lavage

If going through the sheath to the fetlock: 3 cm incision, between two branches of the medial interosseus, then 3 L via a dorsal needle.

Compartments

Two compartments. An outer and an inner proximally, joined only in the common distal compartment — which is why closed irrigation fails. Sheaths of III and IV do not communicate, but proximally in the lower third of the cannon they are separated by a thin wall that must not be incised.

Watch out

Sheaths of III and IV are separated proximally by a thin wall that must not be incised. Lavaging the joint while the sheath is infected is a guaranteed failure.

tap
Budras p. 130 and Fig. 22.1 · Kofler & Martinek, Vet J 2005 · Bertagnoli et al., Vet J 2012 · Hund, Senn & Kofler, Animals 2020 (CC BY)

Pelvic limb4 joints · 1 sheath · 1 bursa
1
HipCoxofemoral

Palpate

The greater trochanter is the only anchor. Gluteal atrophy supports it. Effusion is not palpable at all.

Needle

Spinal 16–18 G, 15 cm+ in an adult. Dorsolateral — dorsal to the greater trochanter, directed ventromedially.

Then

Lavage possible but difficult, under epidural. Arthrotomy is not a field procedure.

Compartments

Simple joint, one cavity. Ligaments: transverse acetabular, acetabular labrum, ligament of the head of the femur. No accessory ligament in cattle, unlike the horse.

Watch out

Avoid any caudal approach — sciatic nerve. Strike the acetabular rim → angle distally. Without ultrasound this is guesswork.

tap
Nuss et al., Arthrocentesis in cattle Pt 2, 2002 · Nuss, SAT 2003 · Grubelnik et al., BMTW 2002 · Budras p. 29
2
StifleFemoropatellar + femorotibial

Palpate

Patella, the three patellar ligaments, the tibial tuberosity. The lateral parapatellar pouch fills first.

Needle

16–18 G, 7–9 cm. Femoropatellar: 4 cm proximal to the tibial tuberosity, between the medial and middle patellar ligaments.

Then

Lateral femorotibial: in the extensor groove of the tibia, on the border of the peroneus tertius tendon.

Lavage

2–4 L · epidural; GA in calves

Compartments

Three sacs, partly joined. Femoropatellar and medial femorotibial always communicate. All three communicate in only about 57% of limbs. The lateral femorotibial communicates with nothing — always a separate procedure.

Watch out

FP and medial FT always communicate; all three only in ~57%. The lateral FT communicates with nothing — treat it as a separate procedure.

tap
Nuss et al., Pt 2, 2002 · Desrochers 1995 · Desrochers et al. 1996 · Budras p. 28 and Fig. 28.1
3
TarsocruralTibiotarsal

Palpate

Run from the distal end of the lateral malleolus forward to the cranial edge of the tibia, to where the talus is felt.

Two plantar recesses lie in the angle between the distal caudal tibia and the cranial calcaneus.

Needle

16–18 G. The dorsomedial recess is the optimal diagnostic site; dorsolateral is the best scope portal.

Then

Plantarolateral into the plantar recess — and from here lavage works in the standing animal.

Lavage

1.5–3 L · IVRA + IVRLP · at least two diagonal recesses, cranial to caudal

Compartments

Openly conjoined with the proximal intertarsal joint (Budras) — yet Nuss advises entering it separately from dorsomedially, because fibrin blocks the connection. Do both: expect them joined, treat them separately. The distal tibiofibular joint also drains into the tarsocrural.

Watch out

Enter the proximal intertarsal joint separately, dorsomedially, between the medial collateral ligament and the medial branch of the cranial tibial tendon. Only 56% of tarsitis cases recover — say so before you start.

tap
Nuss et al., Pt 2, 2002 · Lardé et al., Vet Surg 2017 · Kofler & Altenbrunner-Martinek 2008 · Meier 1997 · Budras pp. 28, 135
4
Distal tarsal jointsCentrodistal · tarsometatarsal

Palpate

Narrow spaces on the dorsomedial distal tarsus. Swelling barely visible — this is a radiographic diagnosis.

Needle

20 G short, dorsomedial, perpendicular to the long axis. Expect drops only.

Then

TMT communicates with the distal intertarsal. Tarsocrural does not reach either — lavaging it does nothing down here.

Lavage

Too small for meaningful through-and-through lavage. Aim is a sample.

Compartments

Tarsometatarsal communicates with the distal intertarsal. There is no open communication between the tarsocrural and the tarsometatarsal — lavaging the hock from above does nothing down here.

Watch out

Most lesions here are bone, not synovium — osteomyelitis or sequestrum, and in old cows “bovine spavin”. Two orthogonal films beat a tap.

tap
Nuss et al., Pt 2, 2002 · Kofler et al., Vet Clin Food Anim 2014 · Budras pp. 28, 135
5
Tarsal sheathDeep flexor sheath at the tarsus

Palpate

Elongated distension medioplantar to the calcaneus, running proximodistally. Equine equivalent: thoroughpin.

Needle

18 G into the most distended part of the lumen, under ultrasound, avoiding the medial saphenous vessels.

Then

Second portal at the opposite end of the lumen.

Lavage

1–2 L ± povidone-iodine 0.1%

Compartments

Sheath of m. flexor digitorum lateralis and m. tibialis caudalis, separate from the tarsal joints. Dorsally, the digital extensor sheaths communicate partially with the cranial tibial sheath and the peroneus tertius bursa; the medial digital flexor has a sheath of its own.

Watch out

In 97 cattle with a swollen hock the differentials included vein thrombosis. Puncturing a thrombosed vein is an avoidable error.

tap
Nuss & Maierl, TPG 2000 · Kofler & Altenbrunner-Martinek, BMTW 2008 · Budras p. 28
6
Calcanean bursaeSubtendinous · subcutaneous

Palpate

Subtendinous (retrocalcaneal) — constant, between SDF tendon and the gastrocnemius insertion on the tuber calcanei. The one that matters.

Lumen runs 9 cm proximal and 7 cm distal to the tuber; lies 8–10 mm under the skin.

Talks to the bursa tendinis calcanei in 100% of cases, to the subcutaneous bursa in 39%.

Needle

Via the wound tract after debridement, or into the effusion under a 7.5 MHz probe. Sedate 0.05 mg/kg xylazine, tourniquet 15 cm above the tuber, procaine into the lateral plantar metatarsal vein.

Then

Three drainage portals, 15 mm each: proximolateral, proximomedial, laterodistal. Curette through each.

Lavage

2–3 L saline + povidone-iodine 0.1% · drain + foam · 5 ml ampicillin · relavage 2–3×/week for 3 weeks

Compartments

Subtendinous bursa communicates with the bursa tendinis calcanei in 100% of cases, and with the subcutaneous bursa in 39%. Lumen runs 9 cm proximal and 7 cm distal to the tuber, lying 8–10 mm under the skin.

Watch out

Scan first for SDF rupture and a rough contour of the tuber. Bursitis alone: 15/18 recovered, median survival 23 months. With bone involvement it is a different disease.

tap
Kofler & Sullmann, Animals 2021;11:1446 (CC BY) · Kofler, Erlacher & Pagliosa, TPG 2019 · Budras pp. 28, 135–136

Volumes

What you can aspirate

A normal joint gives drops, sometimes nothing, even when the needle is perfectly placed. Most generous: tarsocrural and stifle. Least: the distal tarsal joints, where a single drop on the hub is the whole sample.

So a thin yield from a non-swollen joint means nothing. A thin yield from a distended joint is a finding.

Range reflects joint size and animal size. Stop when the efflux is clear, not when the bag is empty.

Joint capacity in mL is not published for cattle. I found distension figures only for the horse — tarsocrural 60 ml, antebrachiocarpal 25, middle carpal 20, fetlock 14–20, DIP 12–15, PIP 7–10, DFTS 42, subtendinous calcaneal bursa 15, navicular bursa 10. Useful for order of magnitude only. Do not treat these as cattle numbers.

Read it on the spot

Serous · serofibrinousClear to slightly turbid, small clots. Lavage.
FibrinousHeavy gelatinous fibrin. Arthrotomy — needles will not clear it.
PurulentArthrotomy, resection, or cull.
Any turbidity = infection. In cattle, inflammatory joint disease is septic until proven otherwise.

Three tailgate tests

Clarity

Normal is colourless to pale yellow and you can read print through it.

String

Let a drop fall from the hub. Normal draws a string of about 2 cm. Falls like water = hyaluronan gone = inflammation.

Clot

Turbid fluid that clots completely within about 15 min is fibrinous arthritis.

A dry tap is a finding

Fibrin can be solid enough that nothing aspirates. On ultrasound it is hypo- to echoic with no flow. A dry tap in a distended joint is positive, not negative.

Inject 2–3 ml saline and re-aspirate — usually enough for cytology and culture.

First sample only. Repeat taps and a single lavage change the cytology themselves.

Drugs

Sedation

Xylazine 0.05 mg/kg, lateral recumbency.

Fetlock · carpus · tarsus · digit

IVRA + IVRLP. Tourniquet on MC/MT III and IV; inject the cranial branch of the saphenous vein, or any larger vein distal to it.

Tourniquet at the calcaneal groove must be padded with coarse calico, or the vessels there stay open and the block fails.
Stifle · hip

Epidural: procaine or lidocaine 0.4 ml/kg, or xylazine 0.1 mg/kg made up to 0.4 ml/kg with saline.

Calves

GA: xylazine 0.1 mg/kg + ketamine 2 mg/kg IV.

The feel

Skin (firm, gritty) → capsule (second distinct resistance, often a soft give) → in (resistance gone, needle swims).

Hold the hub, not the syringe, while advancing. A syringe hides the feel.

In a tight joint fluid wells up on its own — collect that before attaching anything.

Flow stops suddenly? Villi in the bevel. Release, rotate a few degrees, draw again.

Hit bone? Withdraw a few mm, re-angle, advance. Do not fan while deep — it scores cartilage.

Is it open?

Enter through clean intact skin away from the wound. Never through the wound.

Intact joint pressurises — after a few ml it is hard to push. Breached joint never builds pressure, and fluid appears at the wound.

Sheaths resist distension poorly; bandage above and below to narrow the space first.

Kit

  • Two cannulas, 1.6–2.0 mm lumen
  • 1–4 L 0.9% NaCl ± povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Syringes ready before fluid runs
  • Clip, full prep, sterile gloves
Lavage until the efflux is clear and fibrin-free — not until the bag is empty.

After

  • Intra-articular ceftiofur or penicillin
  • Gentamicin collagen sponge, optional
  • Portals may stay open for repeat lavage
  • Relavage judged on gross fluid, weight bearing and swelling

Pick the treatment

Antibiotics aloneHaematogenous only, started within 2–4 days. No clear improvement in 36–48 h → go local.
LavageSerous or serofibrinous with small clots. Two cannulas, 1–4 L.
ArthrotomyClotted fibrin, pus, or a penetrating wound. Same sites as arthrocentesis.
ArthroscopyEarly serous cases only. Needs kit and GA or epidural.
Resection / arthrodesisChronic pus with subchondral bone infection. DIP and PIP routinely; fetlock and carpus only, of the proximal joints.

Always alongside

  • Antibiotics at least 2 weeks, sometimes 4. Cutting it short is how these fail.
  • Proven synovial and bone penetration: penicillin, ampicillin, cephalosporins, aminoglycosides, oxytetracycline, fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides.
  • NSAIDs 3 days — flunixin, ketoprofen, carprofen, meloxicam. Also restores feed intake.
  • Combine systemic with IVRLP of the same drug.

Before you commit

Prognosis turns on: the primary cause, mono- vs polyarthritis, type of effusion, which structure, concurrent osteomyelitis, disease elsewhere.

The tarsus is the outlier. Every treated fetlock, carpal and stifle infection healed in one series — only 56% of tarsitis did. Have that conversation first.
Full orthopaedic exam plus ultrasound before choosing treatment or culling, not after.

Calves

In a calf with septic arthritis, examine the umbilicus.

  • Colostrum within 6 h, and check serum Ig
  • Navel disinfection
  • Avoid lung and gut infection
