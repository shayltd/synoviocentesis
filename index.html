Palpate

  • The two prominences of the greater tubercle, and the notch between them
  • Infraspinatus tendon — every approach is measured off it
  • Muscle atrophy and an abducted limb at rest
  • Effusion cannot be palpated here. The capsule sits under thick muscle

  2. 2

Lavage

  • Simple joint — one cavity

Palpate

  • Flex the carpus — the space opens as a depression just distal to the radius
  • Effusion bulges either side of the extensor tendons
  • Filling also shows at the palmar recess above the accessory carpal bone

Portals 4 portals

  1. 1
    Antebrachiocarpal, dorsal — flexed, from lateral or dorsolateral
  2. 2
    Antebrachiocarpal, palmar pouch — the exit for that compartment
  3. 3
    Midcarpal, dorsal — a separate cavity, so a separate tap
  4. 4
    Midcarpal / CMC, palmar pouch — these two always communicate, so one exit serves both
  5. Count on two per compartment, not two for the carpus. Three cavities means up to six needles if all three are septic

Lavage

  • 1–3 L, two cannulas 1.6–2.0 mm
  • Under IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • Three cavities.
  • — Antebrachiocarpal ↔ midcarpal: rarely
  • — Midcarpal ↔ carpometacarpal: always
  • The antebrachiocarpal cavity includes the accessory carpal bone

Watch out

  • Lavaging the antebrachiocarpal does nothing for the lower two
  • In a calf all three may be septic. Separate taps are the only way to know

4portals

Palpate

  • The dorsoproximal pouch bulges with even slight effusion
  • It lies abaxial to the common digital extensor tendon

Portals 4 portals

  1. 1
    Dorsal pouch — just proximal to the joint space, lateral to the extensor tendon
  2. 2
    Plantar triangle — flex the fetlock, ~2 cm into its centre, axiodistally
  3. 3
    Repeat both on the other digit if it is involved. There is no dorsal connection between lateral and medial, so the pair does not drain the pair

Lavage

  • Fill first, then open the exit. Distend the dorsal pouch, leave the syringe attached, and only then place the second needle opposite. That is what gives true through-flow
  • 1–2.5 L ± povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Or enter the plantar pouch directly

Compartments

  • Capacity 5–10 mL — more than the digit joints
  • Dorsal and plantar pouches join behind the sesamoid bones, not across the front
  • No dorsal connection between lateral and medial. You cannot cross between digits from the front
  • Digits III and IV do join at the incisura intercapitalis — wide enough for infection, too narrow to flush through

Watch out

  • Expect infection to cross between digits. Lavage one side at a time
  • The flexor sheath lies immediately palmar and is often involved too

Palpate

  • Swelling runs along a tendon, above and below the carpus
  • Not the dorsal, joint-centred bulge
  • Have sheaths: ext. carpi obliquus, the digital extensors, flexor carpi radialis
  • Do not: ext. carpi radialis, ulnaris lateralis

  1. 1
    Into the lumen at its widest point on ultrasound, parallel to the tendon fibres
  2. 2
    Second portal at the far end of the same lumen
  3. One pair per sheath. Several sheaths can be involved at once, and they do not communicate

Lavage

  • Small — the lumen sets the volume
  • Septic cases usually need open debridement and drains

Compartments

  • Separate cavities from the joints, and clearly distinct on ultrasound

Watch out

  • A swollen carpus has four causes and four treatments: arthritis, tenosynovitis, precarpal hygroma, periarticular abscess
  • Scan each tendon and each pouch before you choose

Palpate

  • Soft, fluctuant, superficial swelling on the dorsal carpus
  • Runs from the midcarpal joint to just below the metacarpal tuberosity
  • Begins as hairless, hyperkeratotic skin — the cubicle is too short
  • Can reach the size of a man's head

Portals 1 portal

  1. 1
    Central aspiration, strict asepsis. One needle — this is a sampling procedure, not a lavage
  2. Enter from the side, not through the dorsal midline

Lavage

  • Excise when enlarged
  • Do not breach the synovial structures at surgery

Compartments

  • Extra-articular, and usually communicates with nothing beneath
  • Invasion into a sheath or the carpal joint happens, but rarely

Watch out

  • Lameness is mild unless it is infected
  • A bursa can fill because the joint under it is effused. Then it is the symptom, not the disease

Palpate

  • Firm or fluctuant swelling over the point of the elbow or the sternum
  • Pressure lesions, same mechanism as the carpal hygroma
  • The subcutaneous bursa is superficial, on the caudal olecranon
  • The bursa of triceps brachii sits deeper, under the terminal tendon

2portals

Palpate

  • Localised swelling between fetlock and coronary band
  • Usually follows a penetrating interdigital wound
  • A very tight joint. Realistically only enterable when badly affected or distended

Portals 2 portals

  1. 1
    Dorsoabaxial, or side-adjacent, perpendicular to the long axis of the pastern
  2. 2
    An opposing needle, abaxial or axial
  3. Position carefully or use ultrasound. The commonest error is landing in the tendon sheath

Lavage

  • 0.5–1 L until the efflux runs clear
  • IVRA with a tourniquet

Compartments

  • The two pastern joints do not communicate with each other
  • But their dorsal pouches reach the coffin joint pouches
  • So PIP to DIP spread is common, PIP to PIP is not

Watch out

  • The flexor sheath runs directly behind. Easy to tap by mistake
  • Purulent with osteomyelitis means abaxial resection, not lavage

Palpate

  • Painful swelling at the caudal third of the abaxial coronet
  • A draining tract at the dorsal coronary band is near-diagnostic
  • Grade 4–5 lame
  • Usually secondary to white line disease, sole ulcer or interdigital phlegmon

Portals 2 portals

  1. 1
    Dorsal: dorsoproximal to the coronary band at 60°, axial or abaxial to the extensor. Aim at the tip of the opposite claw
  2. 2
    Palmar/plantar to the abaxial collateral ligament
  3. Smaller needle as standard. 14G only when there is pus or heavy effusion

Lavage

  • ~1 L until clear and free of fibrin
  • IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • Capacity about 3 mL. You meet resistance already at 1 mL
  • The dorsal pouch connects with the pastern joint's
  • The navicular bursa lies immediately palmar, between DDFT and navicular bone — a separate cavity that deep sepsis rarely respects

Watch out

  • Lavage suits only acute closed arthritis
  • Fibrin, pus or a penetrating wound means resection or amputation

Palpate

  • Elongated swelling along the palmar fetlock, running proximally
  • Not the round, joint-centred bulge
  • Spans the distal third of the cannon to the coronary band of the heel bulbs

Portals 2 portals

  1. 1
    Proximal: two finger-widths proximal to the dewclaws, at 45° until the first resistance, then a distinct pop as it enters
  2. 2
    Distal: midway between the dewclaws and the coronary band, straight in at 90°
  3. Alternative proximal site: abaxially into the concavity between the tendons and the interosseus, fetlock flexed

Lavage

  • Volume is not the limit. Access to both compartments is

Compartments

  • Two compartments. An outer and an inner, proximally
  • They join only in the common distal compartment
  • Sheaths of III and IV do not communicate
  • Proximally they are separated by a thin wall that must not be incised

Watch out

  • The structure most often mistaken for the fetlock joint
  • Lavaging the joint while the sheath is infected is a guaranteed failure

Palpate

  • Patella, the three patellar ligaments, the tibial tuberosity
  • The lateral parapatellar pouch fills first

Portals 4 portals

  1. 1
    Femoropatellar: 4 cm proximal to the tibial tuberosity, between the medial and middle patellar ligaments
  2. 2
    Also workable: lateral to the patellar ligament, medial to it, or directly behind the patella
  3. 3
    Femorotibial: find the tibial tuberosity and the condyles, then enter lateral or medial to the patellar ligament
  4. 4
    Lateral femorotibial needs its own pair — it communicates with nothing. Published landmark: the extensor groove of the tibia, on the border of the peroneus tertius tendon

Lavage

  • 2–4 L
  • Epidural, or GA in calves

Compartments

  • Capacity 20–60 mL. By far the largest joint
  • Sources disagree. Desrochers: femoropatellar and medial femorotibial communicate always, all three in ~57%. Fanny teaches they are joined in only ~20% of cows
  • Assume separate. That is the assumption that makes you place a second needle
  • The lateral femorotibial communicates with nothing, on both accounts

Watch out

  • Fluid going in and nothing coming out means a non-communicating compartment
  • Osteochondrosis and trochlear-ridge osteomyelitis mimic sepsis in calves

Palpate

  • The “small hole”. A depression you can feel dorsomedially on a standing cow, between the tibia, the tarsal bones and the muscle tendon. That is the site
  • Or trace from the distal end of the lateral malleolus forward to the cranial edge of the tibia, until the talus is felt
  • Dorsal, caudolateral and caudomedial recesses all distend markedly

Portals 3 portals

  1. 1
    Dorsomedial recess — best for aspiration and for flushing. The “small hole” on a standing cow
  2. 2
    Plantarolateral, into the plantar recess. From here lavage works standing
  3. 3
    Proximal intertarsal, separately — dorsomedially, between the medial collateral ligament and the medial branch of the cranial tibial tendon
  4. At least two diagonal recesses, cranial to caudal. Two needles do not flush this joint

Lavage

  • 1.5–3 L
  • Under IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • Openly conjoined with the proximal intertarsal joint, per Budras
  • Yet Nuss advises entering that joint separately, because fibrin blocks the link
  • Do both. Expect them joined, treat them separately
  • The distal tibiofibular joint also drains into the tarsocrural

Watch out

  • Separate entry to the proximal intertarsal: dorsomedially, between the medial collateral ligament and the medial branch of the cranial tibial tendon
  • Only 56% of tarsitis cases recover. Say so before you start

Volumes

What you can aspirate

A normal joint gives drops, sometimes nothing, even when the needle is perfectly placed. Most generous: tarsocrural and stifle. Least: the distal tarsal joints, where a single drop on the hub is the whole sample.

So a thin yield from a non-swollen joint means nothing. A thin yield from a distended joint is a finding.

Range reflects joint size and animal size. Stop when the efflux is clear, not when the bag is empty.

Capacity, in cattle. Coffin joint holds about 3 ml at most, with resistance felt by 1 ml. Fetlock 5–10 ml. Stifle 20–60 ml. These are the figures from Fanny's teaching and they are the only cattle numbers I have — the published literature gives capacities for the horse, not the cow.

Read it on the spot

Serous · serofibrinousClear to slightly turbid, small clots. Lavage.
FibrinousHeavy gelatinous fibrin. Arthrotomy — needles will not clear it.
PurulentArthrotomy, resection, or cull.
Any turbidity = infection. In cattle, inflammatory joint disease is septic until proven otherwise.

Three tailgate tests

Clarity

Normal is colourless to pale yellow and you can read print through it.

String

Let a drop fall from the hub. Normal draws a string of about 2 cm. Falls like water = hyaluronan gone = inflammation.

Clot

Turbid fluid that clots completely within about 15 min is fibrinous arthritis.

A dry tap is a finding

Fibrin can be solid enough that nothing aspirates. On ultrasound it is hypo- to echoic with no flow. A dry tap in a distended joint is positive, not negative.

Inject 2–3 ml saline and re-aspirate — usually enough for cytology and culture.

First sample only. Repeat taps and a single lavage change the cytology themselves.

Drugs

Sedation

Xylazine 0.05 mg/kg, lateral recumbency.

Fetlock · carpus · tarsus · digit

IVRA + IVRLP. Tourniquet on MC/MT III and IV; inject the cranial branch of the saphenous vein, or any larger vein distal to it.

Tourniquet at the calcaneal groove must be padded with coarse calico, or the vessels there stay open and the block fails.
Stifle · hip

Epidural: procaine or lidocaine 0.4 ml/kg, or xylazine 0.1 mg/kg made up to 0.4 ml/kg with saline.

Carpus & radius

Ring block, or high retrograde IV anaesthesia — about 60 ml of local anaesthetic.

Calves

GA: xylazine 0.1 mg/kg + ketamine 2 mg/kg IV.

Distal limb = IVRA (Bier block). Proximal joints = epidural or heavy sedation / GA. There is no middle ground for stifle and hip.

The feel

Skin (firm, gritty) → capsule (second distinct resistance, often a soft give) → in (resistance gone, needle swims).

Hold the hub, not the syringe, while advancing. A syringe hides the feel.

In a tight joint fluid wells up on its own — collect that before attaching anything.

Flow stops suddenly? Villi in the bevel. Release, rotate a few degrees, draw again.

Hit bone? Withdraw a few mm, re-angle, advance. Do not fan while deep — it scores cartilage.

Is it open?

Enter through clean intact skin away from the wound. Never through the wound.

Intact joint pressurises — after a few ml it is hard to push. Breached joint never builds pressure, and fluid appears at the wound.

Sheaths resist distension poorly; bandage above and below to narrow the space first.

Kit

  • Two cannulas, 1.6–2.0 mm lumen
  • 1–4 L 0.9% NaCl ± povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Syringes ready before fluid runs
  • Clip, full prep, sterile gloves
Lavage until the efflux is clear and fibrin-free — not until the bag is empty.

After

  • Intra-articular ceftiofur or penicillin
  • Gentamicin collagen sponge, optional
  • Portals may stay open for repeat lavage
  • Relavage judged on gross fluid, weight bearing and swelling

Pick the treatment

Antibiotics aloneHaematogenous only, started within 2–4 days. No clear improvement in 36–48 h → go local.
LavageSerous or serofibrinous with small clots. Two cannulas, 1–4 L.
ArthrotomyClotted fibrin, pus, or a penetrating wound. Same sites as arthrocentesis.
ArthroscopyEarly serous cases only. Needs kit and GA or epidural.
Resection / arthrodesisChronic pus with subchondral bone infection. DIP and PIP routinely; fetlock and carpus only, of the proximal joints.

Always alongside

  • Antibiotics at least 2 weeks, sometimes 4. Cutting it short is how these fail.
  • Proven synovial and bone penetration: penicillin, ampicillin, cephalosporins, aminoglycosides, oxytetracycline, fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides.
  • NSAIDs 3 days — flunixin, ketoprofen, carprofen, meloxicam. Also restores feed intake.
  • Combine systemic with IVRLP of the same drug.

Before you commit

Prognosis turns on: the primary cause, mono- vs polyarthritis, type of effusion, which structure, concurrent osteomyelitis, disease elsewhere.

The tarsus is the outlier. Every treated fetlock, carpal and stifle infection healed in one series — only 56% of tarsitis did. Have that conversation first.
Full orthopaedic exam plus ultrasound before choosing treatment or culling, not after.

Calves

In a calf with septic arthritis, examine the umbilicus.

  • Colostrum within 6 h, and check serum Ig
  • Navel disinfection
  • Avoid lung and gut infection
