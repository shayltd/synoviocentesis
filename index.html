Palpate

  • Cranial + caudal prominences of the greater tubercle, and the notch between them
  • Infraspinatus tendon — every approach is measured off it
  • Supraspinatus / infraspinatus atrophy; limb abducted at rest
  • Effusion is not palpable — capsule lies under thick muscle
  • Lies close to the bursa subtendinea m. bicipitis brachii

Needle in

  • Spinal needle, caudolateral
  • Enter just caudal to the infraspinatus tendon
  • Aim craniomedially into the caudal recess
  • Scope reference: 1 cm caudal to tendon border, 1 cm proximal to caudal tubercle

Then

  • Second cannula into the same caudal recess

Lavage

  • 1–2 L 0.9% NaCl
  • Two cannulas 1.6–2.0 mm
  • Epidural or GA — not standing

Compartments

  • Simple joint — one cavity, no compartments
  • Intertubercular bursa is separate from the capsule — a full bursa is not a full joint

Watch out

  • Second hardest joint after the hip
  • Suprascapular nerve runs close
  • Never tap a shoulder without ultrasound
  • Scan first to tell bicipital bursitis from shoulder arthritis — they present the same

Palpate

  • Find the palpable depression between the lateral humeral epicondyle and the tuber olecrani — that gap is the target
  • Lateral collateral ligament runs between them

Needle in

  • Enter just caudal to the caudal border of the lateral collateral ligament
  • Direct craniomedially into the radiohumeral space

  • The caudomedial pouch — in sepsis this is not optional

  • Lavage from lateral alone leaves fibrin in the caudomedial pouch

Palpate

  • Flex to 90° — space opens as a dorsal depression just distal to the radius
  • Effusion bulges dorsally and medially, either side of the extensor tendons
  • Filling shows at the palmar recess proximal to the accessory carpal bone

Needle in

  • Flexed, from the lateral or dorsolateral aspect — not straight through the front
  • Avoid the dorsal midline: the bursa precarpalis sits there and is a frequent site of septic bursitis. Going through it can carry infection into the capsule
  • Tap each compartment separately — they are not one joint

Then

  • The palmar pouches — large, and where fibrin and pus collect

Lavage

  • 1–3 L
  • Two cannulas 1.6–2.0 mm
  • IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • Three cavities
  • Antebrachiocarpal rarely talks to the midcarpal
  • Midcarpal and CMC always do
  • ABC cavity includes the accessory carpal bone

Watch out

  • Lavaging the ABC does not lavage the other two
  • In a calf, all three may be septic — separate taps are the only way to know

FetlockMetacarpo-/metatarsophalangeal
19Ggauge

Palpate

  • The dorsoproximal pouch bulges with even slight effusion
  • Abaxial to the common digital extensor tendon

Needle in

  • Dorsal pouch: just proximal to the joint space, lateral to the extensor tendon
  • Or: find the angle between the dorsal margin of P1 and the rounded metacarpal joint surface, insert abaxially under the extensor tendon

Then

  • Plantar triangle, bounded by:
  • — caudal margin of the cannon bone
  • — proximal edge of the abaxial sesamoid
  • — abaxial contour of the interosseus
  • Flex the fetlock, insert ~2 cm into the centre, axiodistally

Lavage

  • Inflate first, then open the exit. Fill the dorsal pouch, leave the syringe attached, then place the second needle in the opposite pouch — that gives you true through-flow
  • 1–2.5 L ± povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Or target the plantar pouch directly in the joint space

Compartments

  • Holds 5–10 mL — more than the digit joints
  • Dorsal and plantar pouches connect behind the sesamoid bones, not across the front
  • No direct dorsal connection between lateral and medial — so you cannot cross between digits from the front
  • Two joints, one slit — digits III and IV each have their own recess
  • Joined at the incisura intercapitalis
  • Wide enough for infection. Too narrow to flush through.

Watch out

  • Expect spread between digits — lavage one side at a time
  • The flexor sheath lies immediately palmar and may be involved too

Palpate

  • Swelling runs along a tendon, above and below the carpus
  • Not the dorsal, joint-centred bulge
  • Have sheaths: ext. carpi obliquus, digital extensors, flexor carpi radialis
  • Do not: ext. carpi radialis, ulnaris lateralis

Needle in

  • Into the sonographic lumen at maximum distension
  • Parallel to the tendon fibres

Then

  • Second portal at the far end of the lumen

Lavage

  • Small — lumen size dictates it
  • Septic cases usually need open debridement and drains

Compartments

  • Separate cavities from the joints — distinct on ultrasound

Watch out

  • A swollen carpus has four causes needing four treatments: arthritis, tenosynovitis, precarpal hygroma, periarticular abscess
  • Scan each tendon and pouch separately

Precarpal bursaHygroma

Palpate

  • Soft, fluctuant, superficial swelling on the dorsal carpus
  • Runs from midcarpal joint to just below the metacarpal tuberosity
  • Starts as hairless hyperkeratotic skin — cubicles too short
  • Can reach the size of a man's head

Needle in

  • Central aspiration, aseptic. Cytology + culture if infection suspected

Then

  • Prove it is extra-articular first — ultrasound or contrast

Lavage

  • Excision when enlarged. Do not breach the synovial structures at surgery

Compartments

  • Extra-articular, and usually communicates with nothing beneath
  • Invasion into a sheath or the carpal joint does happen, rarely

Watch out

  • Lameness is mild unless infected
  • A bursa may fill because the joint under it is effused — then it is the symptom, not the disease

Olecranon & acquired bursaeCapped elbow · presternal
16–18Ggauge
centralaspirate

Palpate

  • Firm or fluctuant swelling over the point of the elbow or the sternum
  • Pressure lesions — same mechanism as the carpal hygroma
  • Subcutaneous olecranon bursa is superficial, on the caudal olecranon
  • Bursa of triceps brachii sits deeper, under the terminal tendon

Needle in

  • Central aspiration after ultrasound confirms fluid
  • Viscous straw-coloured fluid if aseptic — turbidity changes everything

Lavage

  • Excision works well for the chronic fibrous form

Compartments

  • Acquired subcutaneous cavities, no normal communication
  • The triceps bursa is a different structure from the subcutaneous one

Watch out

  • Repeated drainage without fixing the lying surface achieves nothing and risks seeding it
  • Treat the cubicle

PIP — pasternProximal interphalangeal
18–20Ggauge
90°to long axis
tightjoint

Palpate

  • Localised swelling between fetlock and coronary band
  • Usually follows a penetrating interdigital wound
  • A very tight joint — realistically only enterable when badly affected or distended

Needle in

  • Dorsoabaxial, or side-adjacent
  • Needle perpendicular to the long axis of the pastern
  • Position carefully, or use ultrasound — the commonest error is landing in the tendon sheath

Then

  • Opposing needle, abaxial or axial

Lavage

  • 0.5–1 L until the efflux runs clear
  • IVRA with tourniquet

Compartments

  • The two pastern joints do not communicate with each other
  • But their dorsal pouches reach the coffin joint pouches
  • So PIP↔DIP sepsis travels far more readily than PIP↔PIP

Watch out

  • The flexor sheath runs directly behind — easy to tap it instead
  • Purulent with osteomyelitis → abaxial resection, not lavage

2
DIP — coffinDistal interphalangeal
~3 mLcapacity
5 cmlength

Palpate

  • Painful swelling at the caudal third of the abaxial coronet
  • Draining tract at the dorsal coronary band is near-diagnostic
  • Grade 4–5 lame
  • Usually secondary to white line disease, sole ulcer or interdigital phlegmon

Needle in

  • Use a smaller needle as standard. 14G only when there is a lot of fluid or pus
  • Dorsoproximal to the coronary band, 60° to it
  • Dorsal approach: enter the joint space in its dorsal part, between the middle and palmar/plantar thirds of the claw, and aim the needle at the tip of the opposite claw
  • Axial approach: axial to the extensor tendon

Then

  • Palmar/plantar to the abaxial collateral ligament

Lavage

  • ~1 L until clear and fibrin-free
  • IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • A healthy joint holds about 3 mL at most — you feel resistance already after 1 mL
  • Dorsal pouch connects with the pastern joint's
  • Navicular bursa sits immediately palmar, between DDFT and navicular bone
  • Separate cavity — but deep digital sepsis rarely respects it

Watch out

  • Lavage suits only acute closed arthritis
  • Fibrin, pus or a penetrating wound → resection or amputation

3
Digital flexor sheathDFTS
18Ggauge
45°proximal
90°distal

Palpate

  • Elongated swelling along the palmar/plantar fetlock, running proximally
  • Not the round, joint-centred bulge
  • Spans distal third of the cannon to the coronary band of the heel bulbs

Needle in

  • Proximal entry: about two finger-widths proximal to the dewclaws. Advance at ~45° until you meet the first resistance, then pop through into the sheath
  • Distal entry: midway between the dewclaws and the coronary band, straight in at 90°
  • Or abaxially into the concavity between the tendons and the interosseus, fetlock flexed

Then

  • Closed irrigation fails here — plan open debridement and drainage
  • Through the sheath to the fetlock: 3 cm incision, between two branches of the medial interosseus, then 3 L via a dorsal needle

Lavage

  • Volume is not the limiting factor — access to both compartments is

Compartments

  • Two compartments — outer and inner proximally
  • Joined only in the common distal compartment
  • Sheaths of III and IV do not communicate
  • Proximally separated by a thin wall that must not be incised

Watch out

  • The structure most often confused with the fetlock joint
  • Lavaging the joint while the sheath is infected is a guaranteed failure

Palpate

  • Greater trochanter — the only anchor
  • Gluteal atrophy supports it
  • Effusion is not palpable at all

Needle in

  • Ultrasound with a convex probe is strongly recommended — this joint is buried under heavy muscle
  • Long, large-gauge needle proximal to the trochanter major
  • Dorsolateral, directed ventromedially

Then

  • Lavage possible but difficult, under epidural
  • Arthrotomy is not a field procedure

Compartments

  • Simple joint, one cavity
  • Ligaments: transverse acetabular, acetabular labrum, ligament of the head of femur
  • No accessory ligament in cattle, unlike the horse

Watch out

  • Avoid any caudal approach — sciatic nerve
  • Strike the acetabular rim → angle distally
  • Without ultrasound this is guesswork

StifleFemoropatellar + femorotibial
20–60 mLcapacity
4 cmabove tuberosity

Palpate

  • Patella, the three patellar ligaments, the tibial tuberosity
  • Lateral parapatellar pouch fills first

Needle in

  • Femoropatellar: 4 cm proximal to the tibial tuberosity, between the medial and middle patellar ligaments
  • Three workable femoropatellar routes: lateral to the patellar ligament, medial to it, or directly proximal / behind the patella
  • Femorotibial: find the tibial tuberosity and the condyles, then enter the joint space lateral or medial to the patellar ligament

Then

  • Lateral femorotibial: extensor groove of the tibia, on the border of the peroneus tertius tendon

Lavage

  • 2–4 L
  • Epidural; GA in calves

Compartments

  • Holds 20–60 mL — by far the largest
  • Sources disagree on FP ↔ FT: Desrochers found femoropatellar and medial femorotibial communicate always, all three in about 57%. Fanny teaches they are joined in only about 20% of cows.
  • Practically: assume they are separate and confirm with your own flush. The safe assumption is the one that makes you place a second needle.
  • Lateral femorotibial communicates with nothing

Watch out

  • Fluid in and nothing out = you are in a non-communicating compartment
  • Osteochondrosis and trochlear-ridge osteomyelitis mimic sepsis in calves

16–18Ggauge

Palpate

  • The “small hole”: a depression you can feel on a standing cow, dorsomedially, between the tibia, the tarsal bones and the muscle tendon. This is the site.
  • Run from the distal end of the lateral malleolus forward to the cranial edge of the tibia
  • Stop where the talus is felt
  • Two plantar recesses lie in the angle between distal caudal tibia and cranial calcaneus
  • Dorsal, caudolateral and caudomedial recesses all distend markedly

Needle in

  • Dorsomedial recess — the optimal site for both diagnostic aspiration and flushing
  • Dorsolateral is the best scope portal

Then

  • Plantarolateral into the plantar recess
  • From here lavage works in the standing animal
  • At least two diagonal recesses, cranial to caudal

Lavage

  • 1.5–3 L
  • IVRA + IVRLP

Compartments

  • Openly conjoined with the proximal intertarsal (Budras)
  • Yet Nuss says enter that one separately, dorsomedially, because fibrin blocks the link
  • Do both: expect them joined, treat them separately
  • Distal tibiofibular joint also drains into the tarsocrural

Watch out

  • Separate entry to the proximal intertarsal: between the medial collateral ligament and the medial branch of the cranial tibial tendon
  • Only 56% of tarsitis cases recover — say so before you start

Palpate

  • Narrow spaces on the dorsomedial distal tarsus
  • Swelling barely visible — this is a radiographic diagnosis

Needle in

  • Dorsomedial, short needle, perpendicular to the long axis
  • Expect drops only

Then

  • Too small for meaningful through-and-through lavage — the aim is a sample

Compartments

  • Tarsometatarsal ↔ distal intertarsal: yes
  • Tarsocrural ↔ tarsometatarsal: no open communication
  • Lavaging the hock from above does nothing down here

Watch out

  • Most lesions here are bone, not synovium — osteomyelitis or sequestrum
  • In old cows, arthritic exostosis = “bovine spavin”
  • Two orthogonal films beat a tap

Palpate

  • Swelling over the tuber calcanei, usually with an overlying wound
  • In cattle this is a pressure-sore disease — hock lesions first, wounds second
  • Lumen runs 9 cm proximal and 7 cm distal to the tuber

Needle in

  • Via the wound tract after debridement, or into the effusion on a 7.5 MHz probe
  • Sedate xylazine 0.05 mg/kg
  • Tourniquet 15 cm above the tuber, procaine into the lateral plantar metatarsal vein

Then

  • Three drainage portals, 15 mm each:
  • — proximolateral
  • — proximomedial
  • — laterodistal
  • Curette exudate out through each

Lavage

  • 2–3 L saline + povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Drain + foam, 5 ml ampicillin
  • Relavage 2–3×/week for about 3 weeks

Compartments

  • Subtendinous ↔ bursa tendinis calcanei: 100%
  • Subtendinous ↔ subcutaneous: 39%
  • Lies only 8–10 mm beneath the skin

Watch out

  • Scan first for SDF rupture and a rough contour of the tuber
  • Bursitis alone: 15/18 recovered, median survival 23 months
  • With bone involvement it is a different disease

Volumes

What you can aspirate

A normal joint gives drops, sometimes nothing, even when the needle is perfectly placed. Most generous: tarsocrural and stifle. Least: the distal tarsal joints, where a single drop on the hub is the whole sample.

So a thin yield from a non-swollen joint means nothing. A thin yield from a distended joint is a finding.

Range reflects joint size and animal size. Stop when the efflux is clear, not when the bag is empty.

Capacity, in cattle. Coffin joint holds about 3 ml at most, with resistance felt by 1 ml. Fetlock 5–10 ml. Stifle 20–60 ml. These are the figures from Fanny's teaching and they are the only cattle numbers I have — the published literature gives capacities for the horse, not the cow.

Read it on the spot

Serous · serofibrinousClear to slightly turbid, small clots. Lavage.
FibrinousHeavy gelatinous fibrin. Arthrotomy — needles will not clear it.
PurulentArthrotomy, resection, or cull.
Any turbidity = infection. In cattle, inflammatory joint disease is septic until proven otherwise.

Three tailgate tests

Clarity

Normal is colourless to pale yellow and you can read print through it.

String

Let a drop fall from the hub. Normal draws a string of about 2 cm. Falls like water = hyaluronan gone = inflammation.

Clot

Turbid fluid that clots completely within about 15 min is fibrinous arthritis.

A dry tap is a finding

Fibrin can be solid enough that nothing aspirates. On ultrasound it is hypo- to echoic with no flow. A dry tap in a distended joint is positive, not negative.

Inject 2–3 ml saline and re-aspirate — usually enough for cytology and culture.

First sample only. Repeat taps and a single lavage change the cytology themselves.

Drugs

Sedation

Xylazine 0.05 mg/kg, lateral recumbency.

Fetlock · carpus · tarsus · digit

IVRA + IVRLP. Tourniquet on MC/MT III and IV; inject the cranial branch of the saphenous vein, or any larger vein distal to it.

Tourniquet at the calcaneal groove must be padded with coarse calico, or the vessels there stay open and the block fails.
Stifle · hip

Epidural: procaine or lidocaine 0.4 ml/kg, or xylazine 0.1 mg/kg made up to 0.4 ml/kg with saline.

Carpus & radius

Ring block, or high retrograde IV anaesthesia — about 60 ml of local anaesthetic.

Calves

GA: xylazine 0.1 mg/kg + ketamine 2 mg/kg IV.

Distal limb = IVRA (Bier block). Proximal joints = epidural or heavy sedation / GA. There is no middle ground for stifle and hip.

The feel

Skin (firm, gritty) → capsule (second distinct resistance, often a soft give) → in (resistance gone, needle swims).

Hold the hub, not the syringe, while advancing. A syringe hides the feel.

In a tight joint fluid wells up on its own — collect that before attaching anything.

Flow stops suddenly? Villi in the bevel. Release, rotate a few degrees, draw again.

Hit bone? Withdraw a few mm, re-angle, advance. Do not fan while deep — it scores cartilage.

Is it open?

Enter through clean intact skin away from the wound. Never through the wound.

Intact joint pressurises — after a few ml it is hard to push. Breached joint never builds pressure, and fluid appears at the wound.

Sheaths resist distension poorly; bandage above and below to narrow the space first.

Kit

  • Two cannulas, 1.6–2.0 mm lumen
  • 1–4 L 0.9% NaCl ± povidone-iodine 0.1%
  • Syringes ready before fluid runs
  • Clip, full prep, sterile gloves
Lavage until the efflux is clear and fibrin-free — not until the bag is empty.

After

  • Intra-articular ceftiofur or penicillin
  • Gentamicin collagen sponge, optional
  • Portals may stay open for repeat lavage
  • Relavage judged on gross fluid, weight bearing and swelling

Pick the treatment

Antibiotics aloneHaematogenous only, started within 2–4 days. No clear improvement in 36–48 h → go local.
LavageSerous or serofibrinous with small clots. Two cannulas, 1–4 L.
ArthrotomyClotted fibrin, pus, or a penetrating wound. Same sites as arthrocentesis.
ArthroscopyEarly serous cases only. Needs kit and GA or epidural.
Resection / arthrodesisChronic pus with subchondral bone infection. DIP and PIP routinely; fetlock and carpus only, of the proximal joints.

Always alongside

  • Antibiotics at least 2 weeks, sometimes 4. Cutting it short is how these fail.
  • Proven synovial and bone penetration: penicillin, ampicillin, cephalosporins, aminoglycosides, oxytetracycline, fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides.
  • NSAIDs 3 days — flunixin, ketoprofen, carprofen, meloxicam. Also restores feed intake.
  • Combine systemic with IVRLP of the same drug.

Before you commit

Prognosis turns on: the primary cause, mono- vs polyarthritis, type of effusion, which structure, concurrent osteomyelitis, disease elsewhere.

The tarsus is the outlier. Every treated fetlock, carpal and stifle infection healed in one series — only 56% of tarsitis did. Have that conversation first.
Full orthopaedic exam plus ultrasound before choosing treatment or culling, not after.

Calves

In a calf with septic arthritis, examine the umbilicus.

  • Colostrum within 6 h, and check serum Ig
  • Navel disinfection
  • Avoid lung and gut infection
